# My Bali — Provider Ecosystem, Transport Operations, and Verification

**Status:** Working principles and founder direction; category SOPs, legal review, insurance, and operational rules remain to be designed.

## 1. Core positioning: local-first, Bali-focused
My Bali is intended first to serve people who live in Bali and their everyday needs. Visitors from other parts of Indonesia and international travelers are welcome, but the product's first responsibility is to build a dependable Bali-based ecosystem.

The platform aims to connect users to genuine local businesses and operators, with clear identity, location, service, pricing, and fulfillment responsibility. Trust and quality take priority over rapid, uncontrolled listing growth.

## 2. Direct-provider and physical-presence principle
The preferred provider is the actual business, owner, manager, or duly authorized operator responsible for the service. Listings should not misrepresent brokers, unauthorized resellers, lead generators, or businesses that have no verifiable operating presence.

For categories that require a physical storefront or operating location, applicants must provide a real Bali address and evidence appropriate to the category. My Bali may use document checks, location checks, site visits, customer reports, and ongoing monitoring. A listing that is materially false, fictitious, or cannot be found at its represented location may be suspended or removed after a fair review process.

Some legitimate services are mobile or appointment-based and may not have a customer-facing shop. These need category-specific location and authorization rules rather than a blanket storefront requirement.

A person must not be presented as the actual operator if they only resell or pass leads. Any permitted agency, franchise, distribution, or partner relationship must be authorized and transparently disclosed.

## 3. Provider categories and marketplace scope
Potential categories include:
- Food: restaurants, cafes, warungs, bakeries, food stalls, and catering.
- Shopping: boutiques, fashion, souvenirs, crafts, gifts, and selected retail goods.
- Transport: taxi, motorcycle, car, minivan, bus, airport transfer, and local operators.
- Courier/delivery: documents, keys, parcels, personal items, food, and eligible retail purchases.
- Stay: hotels, villas, resorts, homestays, and other accommodation.
- Trip and activity: tours, guides, attractions, watersports, diving, trekking, and experiences.
- Spa and lifestyle: spa, massage, salon, barber, tattoo studios, wellness, and personal care.
- Local services: cleaning, laundry, repairs, technicians, and other approved services.
- Events and property may be considered as separate categories.

The marketplace is curated, not an unrestricted classifieds platform. Used-goods listings and peer-to-peer vehicle sales are not part of the intended core scope. Gadget sales and other higher-risk retail categories remain open for explicit policy decisions. New, legitimate retail goods from verified Bali businesses may be considered. Category admission rules should protect the My Bali brand and user trust.

## 4. Provider registration and verification
Registration may be accessible, but publication, transaction access, and trust status should depend on the checks required for the category and risk.

Verification may include:
- Identity of the owner or authorized representative.
- Business identity and authority to operate or represent the business.
- Actual operating address, service area, and contact route within the platform.
- Category-specific licenses or permits where legally required.
- Real photos, products, services, prices, and operating information.
- Site visit or additional review for higher-risk categories or suspicious cases.

Verification is not a lifetime guarantee. Information must be kept current and may be rechecked. If false information or a fictitious location is confirmed, My Bali may restrict, suspend, or remove the listing, preserve relevant evidence, and prevent repeat registration where lawful and proportionate. Providers should have notice and an appeal route except where urgent action is necessary to protect users or comply with law.

## 5. Transport ecosystem: two participation routes
### A. Independent / freelance providers
Independent drivers may be allowed for suitable, lower-risk journeys and deliveries. They are not automatically equivalent to a managed vendor. Their identity, vehicle, documents, and eligibility still require checks. The user interface must clearly explain their status, what checks have been completed, and which protections do or do not apply.

Potential use cases include simple local transport or low-risk errands, subject to category rules. High-value, sensitive, or higher-risk deliveries may require a vendor-managed service or additional safeguards.

### B. Vendor-managed operators
A vendor registers as an operator, defines its service territory and fleet/driver management responsibility, and submits evidence of its driver-selection and operating SOPs. My Bali verifies the vendor and establishes minimum platform standards. The vendor remains responsible for its internal driver selection and operational management, while My Bali monitors platform compliance and user outcomes.

Multiple vendors may serve the same territory. The platform should compare eligible offers using proximity, estimated pickup time, service type, availability, vehicle suitability, quality, and price—not simply vendor priority or payment.

The vendor's own payment split with its drivers is its operational matter, subject to applicable law and transparent obligations. My Bali's platform fee/commission is separate. The founder's direction is to keep My Bali's own take rate modest so vendors are not pressured into excessive driver deductions; the platform should not claim to control vendor-driver deductions unless a specific agreement gives it that responsibility.

### Transport companies and existing operators
Established Bali operators and larger companies may participate if they meet My Bali requirements. No operator receives an automatic exemption based on brand recognition. They must follow applicable platform rules for pricing, driver/vehicle identity, service status, complaints, and transaction records.

## 6. Transport quality and fulfillment
For motorbike, car, and other transport categories, the platform should record the actual approved driver and vehicle. Relevant checks may include:
- Driver identity and eligibility.
- Vehicle registration/plate match.
- Vehicle make, model, year, and condition against category standards.
- Insurance and permits where required.
- No unapproved substitution of driver or vehicle.
- Trip status, pickup/drop-off confirmation, and complaint evidence.

The desired standard is reliable, well-maintained, relatively modern vehicles, but exact age limits and inspection criteria must be defined by category and legal/operational feasibility.

Courier workflows should record item category, pickup and delivery parties, handover evidence, status, and proof of receipt. Higher-value or sensitive goods need enhanced handling rules, limits, and potentially insurance. Do not promise insurance or compensation until coverage, exclusions, claims evidence, and an actual provider/underwriter are established.

## 7. Territory and availability
Vendors may define one or more service territories, such as districts or zones. Territories may overlap; multiple eligible providers can compete in the same area. The user should see which provider is actually fulfilling the service and the expected pickup/arrival time.

Instant and same-day delivery are desired priorities for Bali-local commerce, but must be presented only when a suitable provider and route are available. The platform must not promise same-day service when capacity, distance, traffic, operating hours, or item restrictions make it unrealistic.

## 8. Pricing and local-user parity
The founder's direction is that providers should set and update their own prices, with My Bali displaying the current provider-supplied price and its last-updated context. My Bali should not arbitrarily invent provider prices.

The intended principle is equal provider pricing for local and foreign customers, except where a price distinction is legally mandated or officially established, such as certain regulated attraction admission categories. Any such difference must be explained transparently and supported by the applicable rule—not invented by the provider or platform.

Estimated prices, provider-confirmed quotes, and final transaction amounts must be clearly distinguished.

## 9. Shopping and Bali-origin retail
My Bali Shopping is intended to help users find genuine Bali-based stores and buy directly from them. A store's location and actual operating presence should be verifiable. The platform should avoid becoming an unrestricted second-hand classifieds marketplace.

A customer who leaves Bali should still be able to find and buy eligible products from the original Bali store. Delivery outside Bali may take longer; instant/same-day delivery is a Bali-local service objective, not a promise for inter-island orders.

Product origin, seller location, stock, shipping origin, and fulfillment responsibility should be represented honestly. A seller must not claim Bali origin merely by using Bali imagery or keywords.

## 10. Provider content, updates, and discovery
A provider page may contain:
- Verified business identity and operating authority.
- Physical location or approved service area.
- Actual services, products, facilities, and access information.
- Current prices, terms, hours, stock/availability, and update timestamps.
- Authentic photos and videos.
- Announcements, events, and time-limited offers.
- Booking, delivery, and support conditions.

A stable provider page is the source of business information; posts are time-based updates. Relevant updates may reach users who do not follow the provider, based on interest and relevance. Ranking should consider quality, freshness, relevance, frequency, user controls, and fairness. Paid placement, if introduced, must be clearly labeled.

## 11. In-platform communication and transactions
My Bali's intended protected transaction flow should keep discovery, offers, booking, payment, fulfillment, and dispute records connected inside the platform. The platform may detect attempts to move transactions off-platform, such as sharing phone numbers or external payment instructions in contexts where this creates fraud risk.

Controls must be proportionate. Users and providers still need safe ways to coordinate. Do not block legitimate operational messages or emergency contact workflows. A user who independently visits a public physical location and chooses to transact outside My Bali is outside the platform's transaction protections; explain this clearly without implying the platform can control offline conduct.

Supported in-person purchases may still be recorded and paid/confirmed through My Bali, for example by claiming a time-limited offer and presenting a QR credential at the venue.

## 12. Quality enforcement and appeals
Identity verification and service quality are separate. Ongoing quality review may consider accurate pricing, fulfillment, cancellations, complaints, safety, authenticity, and policy compliance.

Possible actions include request for information, correction, warning, feature restriction, suspension, removal, and prevention of repeat abuse where lawful. Enforcement should be evidence-based, proportionate, documented, and appealable. One negative review alone should not automatically remove a provider.

## 13. Open questions
- Category-specific evidence and permit requirements.
- Storefront versus mobile/appointment-based provider rules.
- Exact driver and vehicle age/condition thresholds.
- Vendor onboarding, territory overlap, service-level agreements, and audit rights.
- Independent-driver eligibility and higher-risk job restrictions.
- Insurance, liability, claims, and compensation.
- Provider commission, payment fees, settlement, and vendor-driver economics.
- Product categories allowed or prohibited in My Bali Shopping.
- Same-day/instant coverage and operational capacity.
- Off-platform communication controls and emergency exceptions.
- Suspension, evidence retention, appeals, and repeat-registration rules.
