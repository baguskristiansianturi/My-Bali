# My Bali — Product Concept

**Status:** Exploratory concept; not a finalized specification  
**Owner:** Bagus Kristian Sianturi  
**Geographic focus:** Bali

## 1. Concept
My Bali is envisioned as a connected Bali discovery, travel-planning, provider marketplace, booking, and experience platform. It is not intended to be only an itinerary tool, listing directory, ticketing app, or social network. The value comes from connecting these experiences.

## 2. First-user experience and cold start
The founder intends to be the first user. The product must not show a misleading empty shell simply because no providers or other users have joined yet.

Potential sources of useful first-day content:
- Curated destination and general Bali information maintained by My Bali.
- Clearly identified official provider pages once providers are verified.
- Practical discovery categories and search.
- Personal planning tools, wishlists, and private notes.
- Honest community empty states that explain the community is still growing.

Do not fabricate posts, users, reviews, transactions, popularity, or online presence. General destination information must not be presented as an official provider claim.

## 3. User interests and discovery
The product may ask new users to select interests (for example beaches, food, culture, shopping, nature, or activities). This is a starting signal, not a permanent restriction. Users should be able to change preferences and explore beyond their selected interests.

Recommendations may consider explicit interests, searches, follows, likes, saves, clicks, prior interactions, location context where permitted, freshness, and relevance. The exact algorithm is undecided. Paid placement must be clearly disclosed and must not masquerade as neutral relevance.

## 4. Trip planning
Users may enter arrival/departure dates and times, airport or port, accommodation, interests, desired places, budget, and practical constraints. The system may suggest an itinerary based on travel time, operating hours, visit duration, availability, and route logic. It should explain conflicts and offer alternatives rather than silently forcing a plan.

## 5. Provider pages and updates
A verified provider may have a dedicated business/place page containing its identity, location, services, current prices, operating information, photos, videos, and update timestamps.

Provider content may include announcements, events, new attractions, current conditions, and promotions. A provider may build followers and receive likes or other engagement. Updates may be recommended to relevant users even when they do not follow the provider, subject to relevance, quality, frequency, and advertising rules.

A provider page is the stable source of business information; posts are time-based updates. A post should not silently replace permanent pricing or terms without clear context.

## 6. Booking and transaction
The concept includes booking and payment within My Bali for supported services. An attraction ticket example includes purchase, schedule/validity, transaction record, a QR booking credential, provider-side booking visibility, scanning at arrival, and redemption status.

This is a target concept only. Payment gateway, settlement, refund, cancellation, fraud prevention, QR security, offline operation, and accounting are not yet specified.

## 7. Travel record and community
Users may keep plans private, record visits, and publish selected experiences. Planning, discussion, bookings, payments, and completed visits must remain distinct. Users may follow providers and other people; community features such as chat or live broadcasts are future possibilities, not initial commitments.

## 8. Example: Emma
Emma enters her travel dates, arrival time, and accommodation. She chooses interests, saves places, and receives a suggested itinerary. My Bali identifies schedule conflicts and offers alternatives. She books a supported attraction, pays, receives a booking credential, visits the attraction, and has the booking/visit status reflected in her travel record. She chooses what to keep private and what to share.

## 9. Open scope
The exact launch scope, destination data strategy, provider onboarding, ranking, booking, payment, age policy, moderation, revenue model, and architecture remain undecided.
