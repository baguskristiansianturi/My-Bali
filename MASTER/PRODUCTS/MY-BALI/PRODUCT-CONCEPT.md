# My Bali — Product Concept

**Status:** Exploratory concept; founder direction captured, detailed specifications and launch decisions remain open.  
**Owner:** Bagus Kristian Sianturi  
**Geographic focus:** Bali  
**Strategic principle:** Local-first, Bali-focused, trusted ecosystem.

## 1. Core concept
My Bali is a connected platform for everyday local needs, provider discovery, shopping, mobility, delivery, travel planning, booking, transactions, and recorded experiences. It is built first for people who live in Bali. Domestic and international visitors are welcome, but they are not the first product priority.

My Bali is not simply an itinerary app, ride-hailing app, food marketplace, directory, ticketing product, or social network. Its value comes from connecting these needs through one trusted Bali-based ecosystem.

The founder's ambition is to make My Bali a platform that residents trust and use for everyday life in Bali. Competing with established platforms in Bali is an aspiration to validate, not an assumed outcome.

## 2. Local-first user needs
The first user journey should begin with real daily needs experienced by the founder as a Bali resident:
- Getting a ride or arranging transport.
- Sending an urgent document, key, phone, or eligible parcel.
- Ordering food or arranging pickup.
- Finding a local shop, boutique, or product.
- Finding a spa or personal service.
- Discovering local offers and places.
- Planning trips and activities when needed.

The product must remain useful outside tourist seasons. A resident should have a reason to open My Bali even when not planning a holiday.

## 3. Product family (working names)
Potential service families inside one app:
- **My Bali Food:** restaurants, cafes, warungs, food orders, pickup, and delivery.
- **My Bali Shopping:** verified Bali stores, boutiques, products, gifts, souvenirs, and eligible retail goods.
- **My Bali Kurir:** documents, keys, parcels, personal items, and eligible urgent deliveries.
- **My Bali Taxi:** motorcycle, car, taxi, local rides, and other passenger transport.
- **My Bali Spa:** spa, massage, salon, barber, wellness, and personal care.
- **My Bali Trip:** tours, guides, itinerary, activities, and travel experiences.
- **My Bali Stay:** hotels, villas, resorts, and accommodation.
- **My Bali Rental:** motorbike, car, bicycle, and equipment rental.
- **My Bali Activity:** watersports, diving, snorkeling, trekking, surfing, and experiences.
- **My Bali Event:** festivals, cultural performances, events, and tickets.
- **My Bali Service:** cleaning, laundry, repairs, technicians, and local services.
- **My Bali Lifestyle:** tattoo studios and other approved lifestyle services.
- **My Bali Property:** a possible later category requiring separate risk and legal review.

Names, taxonomy, and launch scope are not final. These are service families within one platform, not necessarily separate apps.

## 4. First-user experience and cold start
The founder intends to be the first real user and test the product through actual everyday behavior. The product must not show a misleading empty shell simply because no providers or other users have joined yet.

Potential first-day value:
- Curated Bali information and discovery.
- Useful search and service categories.
- Honest provider availability and empty states.
- Personal planning, saved places, and private notes.
- Clear distinction between verified providers, pending listings, and general information.

Do not fabricate providers, posts, users, reviews, transactions, popularity, inventory, or online presence.

## 5. Users, identity, and experience record
One user identity should work across service families. A user may have profile information, preferences, saved places, bookings, payments, delivery records, and travel experiences.

The concept includes levels, achievements, and reputation for both users and providers. These must be based on meaningful and verifiable behavior, not merely spending, social status, or raw activity volume. Users should control which personal achievements and experiences are publicly visible. Internal trust and safety signals must remain private and access-controlled.

Keep these states distinct:
- Interest or saved plan.
- Discussion or social interaction.
- Booking request.
- Confirmed booking.
- Payment.
- Provider-confirmed fulfillment.
- User-recorded visit or experience.
- Evidence-supported public story.

A payment does not prove attendance; a booking does not prove completion; GPS presence alone does not prove purchase.

## 6. User interests and discovery
The product may ask new users to select interests such as food, beaches, culture, shopping, nature, wellness, activities, and local services. Preferences are editable and should not restrict exploration.

Recommendations may consider declared interests, searches, follows, likes, saves, clicks, prior interactions, location context where permitted, freshness, quality, and relevance. The exact algorithm is undecided. Sponsored content must be labeled and must not masquerade as neutral recommendations.

## 7. Provider marketplace and trust
My Bali is intended to be a curated marketplace of genuine Bali-based businesses and authorized operators, not an unrestricted classifieds platform. Provider identity, authority, real operating presence, service details, and relevant permits should be verified according to category and risk.

A provider may maintain a stable business page with current services, products, prices, hours, location, availability, authentic media, and update timestamps. Posts are time-based updates and should not silently replace permanent business information.

The platform should not allow fictitious stores, false Bali locations, unauthorized resellers, or misleading product-origin claims. Used-goods listings and peer-to-peer vehicle sales are outside the intended core scope; gadget sales and other categories remain open for policy decisions.

## 8. Transport and courier
Transport has two potential participation routes:
- Independent/freelance drivers, with required identity and vehicle checks and clearly explained limits.
- Vendor-managed operators, responsible for selecting and managing their drivers within a declared service territory and My Bali's minimum standards.

Multiple vendors may operate in the same territory. Established local operators and larger companies may participate if they meet the same platform requirements. Matching should consider proximity, pickup time, availability, vehicle/service suitability, quality, and price.

For courier requests, users may describe the item, pickup/drop-off, urgency, and proposed price. Drivers may accept or respond with a counteroffer. The system should record item category, pickup validation, route/status, recipient coordination, and proof of handover. Higher-risk or high-value items require additional safeguards and may be restricted to vendor-managed services. Insurance must not be promised until actual coverage and claims terms exist.

## 9. Pricing and commerce
Providers should set and update their own prices. My Bali displays provider-supplied prices and update context. The interface must distinguish listed prices, system estimates, provider-confirmed quotes, and final transaction amounts.

The founder's direction is equal provider pricing for local and foreign customers except where an official or legal rule requires a difference, such as certain regulated attraction admission categories. Any exception must be transparent and grounded in the applicable rule.

For Bali-local retail and eligible orders, instant or same-day delivery is a desired priority when real capacity allows. After leaving Bali, users may continue buying eligible products from genuine Bali stores; delivery time and shipping origin must be shown honestly.

## 10. Booking, offers, and QR redemption
Some businesses accept walk-ins; others require reservations or waiting lists. My Bali should support both. A user may claim a time-limited offer, schedule an arrival, and receive a QR or equivalent credential for provider validation. A claim is not the same as a completed purchase.

Possible lifecycle:
1. Offer viewed.
2. Offer claimed.
3. Scheduled or reservation requested.
4. Provider confirms where required.
5. User arrives and checks in.
6. Offer or booking is redeemed.
7. Payment is completed/confirmed.
8. Transaction and fulfillment records are closed.

Deposit requirements should be configurable by offer/service and risk; not every claim should require a deposit. Expiry, no-show, cancellation, waiting-list, refund, and dispute rules need explicit design.

## 11. In-platform communication and transaction integrity
The intended protected flow keeps discovery, offer, booking, payment, fulfillment, and dispute records connected in My Bali. The platform may detect attempts to move transactions off-platform, including phone numbers or external payment instructions in contexts where this creates fraud risk.

Controls must be proportionate and should not block legitimate coordination, safety, or emergency communication. Users may independently visit a physical business and transact outside My Bali, but such offline transactions are not represented as My Bali-protected transactions.

## 12. Trip planning and travel
Users may enter dates, arrival/departure, airport or port, accommodation, interests, budget, desired places, and practical constraints. Itinerary suggestions may consider route time, traffic, opening hours, visit duration, availability, and price. The system should explain conflicts and offer alternatives rather than silently forcing a plan.

International and domestic visitors may use My Bali to discover Bali, plan itineraries, compare current provider prices, book supported services, and keep a profile of their experiences. They should not be the only audience the product serves.

## 13. Reputation and quality
Both providers and users may have reputation signals. Provider quality can consider accurate pricing, service fulfillment, vehicle/driver compliance, cancellations, complaints, and policy compliance. User trust can consider confirmed bookings, completion, cancellations, and verified misconduct.

Ratings must not become an automatic punishment system. Evidence, context, response rights, proportionate enforcement, and appeals are necessary. Public reputation must be separated from confidential safety records.

## 14. Physical Bali presence
A future physical office in Bali is a possible trust and operations asset for onboarding, provider/vehicle inspection, customer support, complaints, disputes, and claims. This is a planning direction, not an existing capability or promise.

## 15. Open scope
Launch sequence, exact service families, provider evidence, transport SOPs, insurance, payment and settlement, commission, shopping restrictions, privacy, moderation, age policy, office operations, and system architecture remain to be designed.

## 16. Social-first product identity

**Founder direction captured (2026-11-02):** My Bali's primary product character is social media. It is not a directory or travel-booking product with social features added afterward. The social layer is the main way people discover, share, discuss, and follow life and businesses in Bali; the local provider and service ecosystem is connected inside that experience.

The primary social feed supports mixed photo and video posts. Posts may be about trips, Bali businesses, destinations, food, everyday life, or other permitted topics; a post does not have to be related to a trip. The exact feed ranking and navigation remain design decisions.

The Trip area is a distinct experience within the platform. **Bali Plan** is a Trip sub-area for pre-travel planning: draft itineraries, intended hotels, destinations, restaurants, interests, estimated budgets, and places a user hopes to visit. **Trip Experience** records experiences that have actually occurred. A plan must never be represented as a completed visit.

A user may share a Trip Plan to the main feed, share it only to Stories/Status, share it with selected audiences, or keep it private. Users may also control visibility of saved places and interests independently from general profile visibility. Sharing a plan does not itself create a booking or prove a future visit.

Stories/Status are a separate temporary-sharing surface and should not automatically appear in the permanent feed. Users may choose to cross-post where appropriate. General photo/video posts remain possible outside Trip.

## 17. Evidence-linked experience publishing

My Bali should distinguish ordinary user-authored social content from system-backed activity records. An eligible completed transaction, provider-confirmed booking, or other validated activity may offer the user an option to publish an **Experience Post** linked to that record.

The system-backed facts (such as provider identity, transaction/activity reference, event time, and verified status) must come from the relevant My Bali records and must not be silently rewritten by the user. User-authored media, caption, and optional commentary remain separate from those facts. Corrections to source records require an auditable correction/dispute process, not silent editing. The record must also be privacy-controlled: the user chooses whether to publish it, and sensitive details such as payment credentials, full receipt data, exact private addresses, and order identifiers are not exposed publicly.

A completed payment alone does not prove a physical visit or quality of experience. Experience labels must accurately describe the evidence available (for example, paid order, provider-confirmed visit, booking completed, or user-submitted story). GPS presence alone is not proof of purchase or attendance.

## 18. Copyright and user-generated content

My Bali may use familiar social-media interaction patterns, but it must use its own visual identity, interaction details, brand assets, and original interface composition. Familiar product conventions do not grant permission to copy another platform's protected code, graphics, branding, text, or distinctive creative assets.

Users should upload content they created or have permission to use. The platform should provide clear rights declarations, reporting and takedown procedures, repeat-infringer handling, and an appeal/counter-notice process appropriate to applicable law. Music and other licensed media should be limited to sources for which My Bali has the necessary platform rights. Do not assume that crediting the original creator, cropping, adding a filter, or using a short clip automatically makes reuse lawful.

No platform can guarantee that user uploads will never trigger a copyright complaint. My Bali must have a practical process to receive, assess, restrict, remove, and review disputed content.
