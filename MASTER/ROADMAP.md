# My Bali — Product Roadmap (Living Outline)

**Status:** Directional sequence only. No delivery dates or feature commitments have been approved.

## Stage 0 — Founder-first discovery and product foundation
- Establish My Bali's positioning: local-first, Bali-focused, trusted ecosystem.
- Capture everyday needs from the founder's real use as a Bali resident.
- Define the product family and shared platform foundations.
- Consolidate provider, transport, marketplace, pricing, trust, and UI/UX principles.
- Validate core user problems and provider willingness to participate.
- Define privacy, safety, age suitability, moderation, legal, and operational principles.
- Establish the social-first product structure: Feed, Stories/Status, Trip, Bali Plan, Trip Experience, and Explore.
- Separate general user-generated content from system-backed experience records.

## Stage 1 — Trust and useful local foundation
- Define user identity, profiles, preferences, saved places, and private records.
- Design the mixed photo/video social feed, Stories/Status, audience controls, and guest browsing boundaries.
- Define Trip Plan sharing separately from actual Trip Experience and booking/transaction states.
- Define evidence-linked Experience Posts, source-record correction, audit history, copyright reporting, and content moderation.
- Define provider registration, identity/authority checks, real-location verification, and category-specific evidence.
- Establish verified, pending, restricted, and suspended provider states.
- Define provider support, complaints, appeals, and ongoing review.
- Build useful discovery/search and honest empty states without fabricated activity.
- Define Bali-local service areas, location accuracy, and provider availability.

## Stage 2 — Everyday local service pilots
- Evaluate a narrow pilot around real resident needs, such as My Bali Kurir, My Bali Taxi, My Bali Food, or My Bali Shopping.
- Define independent driver versus vendor-managed transport routes.
- Define vendor territory management and overlapping-provider matching.
- Establish vehicle, driver, store, and product verification.
- Define order/request lifecycle, quote/offer, acceptance, pickup, fulfillment, and proof of completion.
- Pilot with limited, verified providers and measure service quality before expanding.

## Stage 3 — Provider marketplace and local commerce
- Support verified provider pages, real products/services, current prices, availability, and update dates.
- Define approved retail categories and excluded/high-risk categories.
- Support Bali-local instant/same-day fulfillment only where actual capacity allows.
- Support eligible inter-island orders from genuine Bali stores with accurate shipping information.
- Design offers, walk-ins, reservations, waiting lists, QR claims/redemption, and no-show rules.
- Validate commission hypotheses and provider economics.

## Stage 4 — Booking, payment, and transaction foundation
- Design payment integration, settlement, refunds, chargebacks, cancellation, dispute, and provider reconciliation.
- Keep listed price, estimate, confirmed quote, and final transaction amount distinct.
- Design secure QR or equivalent validation, duplicate-scan protection, and offline procedures.
- Establish in-platform communication and transaction integrity controls with safety/emergency exceptions.
- Pilot each transaction type before broader rollout.

## Stage 5 — Broader Bali ecosystem and travel
- Expand to My Bali Stay, Trip, Activity, Spa, Rental, Event, Lifestyle, and other validated categories.
- Build itinerary planning using dates, location, travel time, opening hours, availability, and budget.
- Support domestic and international visitors without displacing local-first priorities.
- Allow users to retain access to eligible Bali stores after leaving the island.

## Stage 6 — Identity, reputation, and community
- Introduce evidence-aware user and provider reputation.
- Add levels and achievements with privacy controls and non-spending-based pathways.
- Distinguish plans, discussions, bookings, payments, fulfillment, visits, and public stories.
- Add reporting, moderation, anti-spam, appeals, and safety operations.
- Evaluate messaging, live features, and public sharing only when operationally supportable.

## Stage 7 — Operational scale
- Establish staff roles, provider support, customer support, moderation operations, incident response, analytics, and service-level expectations.
- Evaluate a physical Bali office for onboarding, inspections, disputes, and claims.
- Build a sustainable revenue model, infrastructure budget, insurance relationships, and operational capacity.
- Delegate through SOPs and team ownership.

## Roadmap discipline
Priorities and stages must be revised using founder discussion, resident/user validation, provider feedback, feasibility, risk, cost, and actual operational capacity. A roadmap entry is not authorization to build. Trust and service quality must not be sacrificed for listing volume or expansion speed.
