# My Bali — Business Model (Working Draft)

**Status:** Founder direction and exploratory model; no final financial, legal, or fee policy approved.

## 1. Strategic positioning: local-first, Bali-focused
My Bali is built first to serve the everyday needs of people who live in Bali. The initial market focus is Bali, not nationwide expansion. Domestic and international visitors are welcome users, but the platform's foundation should be useful to local residents throughout the year.

The intended advantage is a trusted, curated, Bali-based ecosystem connecting residents and visitors to genuine local providers, with transparent prices, reliable fulfillment, and one connected account and transaction system.

The ambition to outperform larger platforms in Bali is a strategic aspiration, not a proven market outcome. It must be validated through availability, quality, price competitiveness, provider coverage, repeat usage, and user trust.

## 2. Value created
- **Residents:** dependable everyday access to transport, courier, food, shopping, spa, local services, and other needs.
- **Bali businesses and operators:** direct visibility, local demand, control of their own information/prices, access to transactions, and fairer platform economics.
- **Domestic visitors:** useful services while in Bali and continued access to genuine Bali stores after returning home.
- **International visitors:** discover providers, plan itineraries, see current provider prices, book supported services, and maintain a Bali experience profile.
- **Local economy:** make genuine Bali businesses easier to find and transact with, while reducing dependence on opaque resellers and unauthorized intermediaries.

## 3. Product family (working names)
The ecosystem may include:
- My Bali Food.
- My Bali Shopping.
- My Bali Kurir.
- My Bali Taxi.
- My Bali Spa.
- My Bali Trip.
- My Bali Stay.
- My Bali Rental.
- My Bali Activity.
- My Bali Event.
- My Bali Service.
- My Bali Lifestyle.
- My Bali Property (scope to be evaluated).

These are service families inside one My Bali platform, not necessarily separate apps. Names and launch scope remain open. Shared foundations should support identity, provider profiles, location, search, pricing, payments, bookings, fulfillment, reviews, reputation, and support.

## 4. Direct-provider value proposition
My Bali prefers the actual local business or authorized operator rather than an opaque reseller. Eligible providers may manage their own verified profiles, products/services, prices, operating hours, availability, offers, and updates.

For relevant retail categories, users should be able to identify the real Bali store and continue purchasing eligible products after leaving Bali. Product origin, seller location, stock, shipping origin, and fulfillment must be represented honestly.

The platform is intended to be curated rather than an unrestricted classifieds site. Used-goods listings and peer-to-peer vehicle sales are outside the intended core scope; gadget sales and other higher-risk categories remain undecided.

## 5. Participation and verification
The founder wants registration to be accessible for both users and genuine providers, while publication and transaction privileges depend on appropriate checks. Providers should have a real operating presence or a clearly approved mobile/appointment-based service model. My Bali may verify identity, authority, location, category permits, service details, and ongoing quality.

No permanent free-listing policy has been approved. Registration, basic profile, ordinary updates, optional promotion, payment processing, commission, and additional provider tools must be evaluated separately.

## 6. Revenue direction and commission hypotheses
The founder's current direction is to avoid high fixed monthly charges and prefer a modest share of completed transactions:
- Around **2% as an initial hypothesis for UMKM/small local providers**.
- A different tier for larger businesses, with **up to 5% as an initial ceiling hypothesis**.

These are discussion figures, not approved prices or published promises. They require financial modelling, legal review, payment-provider costs, tax treatment, refunds, chargebacks, customer support, fraud loss, insurance, and infrastructure costs.

Commission rules should define the eligible transaction base clearly. Taxes, tips, delivery fees, third-party charges, refunds, and discounts may need separate treatment. A low commission does not automatically guarantee lower consumer prices; the platform should measure whether savings reach users and providers.

For transport vendors, My Bali's platform fee is distinct from the vendor's internal payment split with its drivers. The vendor manages its driver economics, subject to law and contractual transparency. My Bali should set minimum service standards and avoid designing platform fees that create pressure for excessive driver deductions.

## 7. Provider-controlled pricing and price transparency
Providers should set and update their own prices. My Bali displays the provider-supplied price, terms, availability, and update context. The system may calculate estimates and compare offers, but must distinguish:
- Provider-listed price.
- System estimate.
- Provider-confirmed quote.
- Final transaction amount.

The founder's direction is equal provider pricing for local and foreign customers, except where a difference is officially or legally required, such as certain regulated attraction admission categories. Any exception must be transparent and grounded in the applicable rule.

## 8. Transactions and fulfillment
Revenue should primarily follow genuine completed value, not mere registration or presence. Possible workflows include:
- Food orders and pickup/delivery.
- Shopping orders with local same-day/instant delivery where feasible and inter-island shipping where needed.
- Courier pickup, tracking, handover, and proof of receipt.
- Taxi/transport offers, dispatch, trip tracking, and completion.
- Spa and service appointment scheduling.
- Trip, activity, accommodation, and attraction booking.
- In-person offer claims validated by QR or equivalent.

The platform must define order lifecycle, payment, settlement, cancellation, refunds, dispute handling, provider reconciliation, and evidence of fulfillment.

## 9. Local delivery and geographic focus
Instant and same-day fulfillment within Bali is a desired differentiator for eligible categories, subject to real provider capacity, distance, traffic, operating hours, and item eligibility. Do not promise delivery speeds that cannot be fulfilled.

After a customer leaves Bali, eligible Bali-based stores may continue selling to them through the platform, with shipping time and origin shown accurately.

## 10. Trust as a commercial asset
The platform's brand depends on verified providers, real locations, accurate prices, identifiable drivers/vehicles, reliable fulfillment, transparent ratings, safe communication, and responsive complaint handling. Growth should not outrun verification and operational capacity.

A physical Bali office is a possible future trust and operations asset for onboarding, inspection, customer support, disputes, and claims. It is a planning concept, not a current operating capability or promise.

## 11. Open financial and commercial questions
- Final commission tiers and transaction base.
- Payment gateway fees and who bears them.
- Provider settlement timing and reserves.
- Refunds, chargebacks, cancellations, disputes, and fraud loss.
- Vendor-driver economics and minimum contractual protections.
- Insurance and liability costs.
- Free versus paid provider features.
- Sponsored placements and their labeling.
- Customer support and physical-office operating costs.
- Unit economics by product family and launch territory.
