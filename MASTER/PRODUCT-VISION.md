# My Bali — Product Vision

**Product owner:** Bagus Kristian Sianturi  
**Portfolio:** Bagusin  
**Product identity:** Independent Bali-focused product  
**Status:** Living vision; evolving through founder discussions

## Why it exists
My Bali begins with the founder's imagination and personal desire to use the product himself. The founder does not need to be a developer or designer to originate a product. His role is to describe needs, experiences, desired journeys, and problems; product and technical work translate those stories into a coherent system.

The founder's first meaningful success may be helping even one person. Potential beneficiaries include visitors, residents, independent drivers, restaurants, accommodation providers, and direct activity operators.

## Product ambition
My Bali is imagined as a connected Bali platform for:
- Discovering places, businesses, attractions, services, and activities.
- Planning practical trips and itineraries.
- Connecting users directly with genuine providers.
- Publishing provider updates and offers.
- Booking and paying for supported services.
- Recording real travel experiences with appropriate evidence.
- Sharing experiences, following providers and people, and discovering useful community content.

The platform may eventually include messaging, live content, and other community capabilities. These are future possibilities, not launch commitments.

## Ownership and independence
My Bali is owned and originated by Bagus Kristian Sianturi. It is part of the Bagusin portfolio but is an independent product. BDM Trans is not its owner or controller. It may participate as one provider if it meets the same rules as others.

## Geographic identity
Bali is the product's defining location. The ambition may be international in terms of users, but My Bali is not currently conceived as a generic multi-city template.

## Founder-first development
The founder intends to be the first user and personally test the early experience. The product should remain useful even when the platform has very few users or providers. Real use, friction, boredom, missing features, abuse attempts, and operational problems are all potential learning inputs.

The founder may also test the provider and platform-administrator roles, but these roles must remain clearly separated in permissions and product behavior.

## Guiding principles
1. Make the product useful from its earliest usable version; do not fabricate community activity.
2. Make it easy for users and genuine providers to join.
3. Prioritize direct providers and transparent service information.
4. Keep plans, discussions, bookings, payments, visits, and completed experiences distinct.
5. Let users control private and public records.
6. Build safety and moderation into the platform.
7. Grow through real usage, feedback, operational learning, and staged improvements.
8. Keep recommendations fair, explainable, and not secretly controlled by commercial payments.

## Mission — working draft
Help people discover, plan, experience, and remember Bali while creating fair opportunities for genuine local providers.

## Public communication boundary
The public profile website may explain the product vision and invite interest. It must not claim that the app, partner onboarding, live booking, payment, or customer support is operational before those capabilities exist.

## Not decided
Legal entity, company structure, trademark, domain, app-store identity, revenue model, initial launch scope, exact age policy, staffing, and technology architecture remain open.
