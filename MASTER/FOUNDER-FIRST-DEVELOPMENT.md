# My Bali — Founder-First Development Method

**Status:** Working method agreed in founder discussions.

## 1. The founder's role
The founder originates the product through imagination, personal needs, experiences, stories, and desired outcomes. The founder is not expected to provide technical specifications or know how to code or design the system.

The product-thinking partner's role is to listen, interpret, ask useful questions, challenge assumptions respectfully, identify risks, suggest options, and translate the discussion into structured product documentation.

## 2. Discussion is two-way
Sharing is not a one-way interview or passive transcription. The discussion should:
- Ask one useful question at a time when clarification is needed.
- Offer alternative approaches and explain trade-offs.
- Challenge weak assumptions without dismissing the founder's intention.
- Allow the founder to disagree, refine, or change direction.
- Conclude with what is understood, what remains open, and what has actually been decided.

The objective is a reasoned outcome, not agreement for its own sake.

## 3. Founder as first user
The founder intends to register and use My Bali as an ordinary user first, then test provider and platform-owner workflows as needed. The system should make role changes explicit and must not silently grant ordinary user content special treatment.

The first release should offer meaningful things to do even with very few registered users and providers. It must not fabricate users, posts, reviews, bookings, or popularity.

## 4. Learning loop
1. Describe an imagined user experience or problem.
2. Translate it into a user journey or product hypothesis.
3. Identify missing information, risks, abuse cases, and operational dependencies.
4. Discuss alternatives.
5. Record the current status: idea, proposal, assumption, decision, or validation task.
6. If confirmed, update the appropriate Master/SOP/roadmap.
7. Implement only when separately authorized and technically ready.
8. Test the result in the real experience and feed observations back into the loop.

## 5. Avoid technical overload
The founder generally wants to discuss the experience and business direction, not hear detailed code or architecture in every conversation. Technical implications should be maintained in the documentation and raised when a decision materially affects user experience, cost, security, privacy, or feasibility.

## 6. Future team operations
As the product grows, founder discussions can become inputs to roadmap planning and team meetings. Decisions, ownership, acceptance criteria, and SOPs should be clear enough for staff to execute without depending on informal memory alone.

The founder anticipates that My Bali may eventually require staff and operational capacity. This does not imply existing Bagusin services will be discontinued.
